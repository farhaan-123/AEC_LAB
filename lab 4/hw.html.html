Answers (n = 15, p = 0.6)

  1. Mean = np = 9; Variance = np(1−p) = 3.6.
  2. P(X = 9) = 0.206598 (about 20.66%).
  3. P(X ≤ 10) = 0.782722 (about 78.27%), so P(X > 10) = 1 − 0.782722 = 0.217278.
  4. The PMF is highest at k = 9 (probability ≈ 0.2066). The tallest bars are in the region k = 8 to 10, around the mean.
  5. Inference: The distribution is unimodal and slightly left-skewed (p > 0.5), with its peak at the mean (9). About 78% of outcomes fall at 10 or fewer successes, and very large (14 or 15) or very small (below 5) counts are rare. Values cluster within roughly one standard deviation (σ ≈ 1.9) of 9.

Answers (λ = 6)

  1. Mean = λ = 6; Variance = λ = 6 (σ ≈ 2.449).
  2. P(X = 8) = 0.103258 (about 10.33%).
  3. P(X ≤ 5) = 0.445680 (about 44.57%).
  4. Comparison: λ = 2 peaks at k = 1 and 2 (≈ 0.2707 each) and is sharply right-skewed. λ = 6 peaks at k = 5 and 6 (≈ 0.1606 each) and is moderately spread. λ = 10 peaks at k = 9 and 10 (≈ 0.1251 each) and is wide and flat. When λ is a whole number the two values k = λ−1 and k = λ tie for the highest bar.
  5. Inference: As λ increases, the distribution shifts to the right (the centre moves to λ), becomes wider (variance = λ, so σ grows from 1.41 to 2.45 to 3.16) and the peak gets lower because the same total probability of 1 is spread over more values. The shape also becomes more symmetric and bell-like, approaching a normal distribution, while small λ gives a skewed shape that piles up near 0.
